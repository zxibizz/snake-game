# Snake (snake-game)

Snakes in an arena, in the browser: no ads, play alone offline or together over the network. Site: `https://snake.games.bubas.cc`.

## Modes

There are always 60 snakes in the arena: players and bots. From the very first second there are giants several thousand long among them — climbing from last place into the top ten and to the crown takes the whole game.

- **Alone** — the world is computed on the device and works offline (PWA):
  - easy — until the player is in the top 20, bots don't hunt them; in the top 20 they do now and then, and the higher the place, the more often (in the top 10 — up to two hunters at once); the first 10 seconds are safe, and the arena edge pushes you back;
  - medium — bots are bolder and smarter, they hunt players below the top 20 as well, and giants turn away early from anyone cutting them off;
  - hard — bots hunt in packs at any place, in the top 10 — up to five hunters at once.

  After a crash you can continue: on easy with 80% of your length, on medium with 40%, on hard from the very beginning.
- **Together** — a room with a 4-digit code (or a `?room=1234` link), up to 8 players; the remaining places up to 60 are bots of the chosen difficulty:
  - "Team" — your snakes pass through each other, everyone against the bots;
  - "Free for all" — you can cut each other off too.
  After a crash you can continue — with the same share of length as alone at the room's level.

Touch controls: a joystick and a lightning-bolt button for a speed boost, both tuned from the pause menu, "Controls" (two tabs, the field stays visible to try them on). Joystick: "Anywhere on the screen" (default) — it starts wherever you touch; switched off — it stands at a chosen spot (tap the place and drag; a touch inside its ring tries it out), is always faintly visible and is grabbed near that spot; size 32–120 px (bigger — finer aim), dead zone 5–60 % of the radius (the finger may wander there without turning the snake), smoothing 0–150 ms (averages a jittery finger within one touch; a new touch turns at once). Boost button: drag it or tap the spot you want, the slider sets the size. Positions are remembered separately for portrait and landscape. On a computer — the mouse or the arrow keys, Space to boost.

## Languages

The interface is localized into 13 languages: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Portuguese (Brazil), Turkish, Russian, Japanese, Korean and Chinese (Simplified). By default the language follows the device: the first supported one in `navigator.languages`, English otherwise (other variants of Portuguese and Chinese get the Brazilian and Simplified ones). The globe next to "?" on the home screen lets the player pick any language or go back to "Device language"; the choice is remembered on the device. `?lang=xx` in the URL picks a language too, `?lang=auto` goes back to the device language.

- `public/i18n.js` — language detection, the picker's list (`LANG_ORDER`), `setLang()`, `t(key, ...args)`, and translation of the static page (`data-t` → text, `data-t-aria` → `aria-label`, plus the title, `<html lang>` and the manifest for installing). It loads before `client.js`, and the page stays hidden until the language is set, so there's no flash of the wrong language.
- `public/lang/<code>.js` — dictionaries; `en.js` is the reference and the fallback. A value is a string or a function of the arguments (numbers, names); missing keys fall back to English. `lang.name` is the language's own name for the picker.
- Skins, snake types, sounds and difficulty levels have ids in the code (`SKINS[i].id`, `TYPES[i].id`, `EAT[i].id`, …); their names live only in the dictionaries. Bot names are a list per language (`bots`); a language without its own list uses the English names. In a room the host's language names the bots.
- Adding a language: copy `public/lang/en.js` and `public/manifest.webmanifest` to `lang/<code>.js` and `manifest.<code>.webmanifest`, translate them, register the language in `LANGS` and `LANG_ORDER` in `public/i18n.js` and add both files to `ASSETS` in `public/sw.js`. `test/i18n.test.js` checks that every language has exactly the English keys, names for every skin, type and sound, a unique name in the picker, and a cached manifest that differs from the English one only in its texts. Check the layout on a 375 px phone: tab and button labels must fit.

Code, comments and docs are in English only; a test fails if Cyrillic shows up anywhere outside `public/lang/ru.js` and `public/manifest.ru.webmanifest`.

## Balance

The game is unhurried: on hard the road to first place is designed to take five minutes or longer. All the numbers are at the top of `public/core.js`.

- **Room to breathe.** An arena of radius 7000, 60 snakes, 4200 bits of food: on a phone screen there's less than one other snake and about twenty bits of food on average.
- **Long bodies** (`bodyLenOf`): for the same mass a snake is 1.5× longer than before `0.14` — more obstacles in the arena and more to cut people off with.
- **Chaos by level.** On easy and medium bots think and turn slower (`react`, `turn`), hunt each other more often (`feud`) and "lapse" in crowds (`slip`, see "Bots") — about 80 snakes drop out per minute on easy (bots lapse anywhere — `slipAny` — and crash into lying bodies too), 40–60 on medium and 7–10 on hard. The screen still isn't littered with remains: a phone screen shows the same ~20 bits of food.
- **The well-fed aren't greedy.** A bot longer than the level's `giantMin` doesn't chase food — it crawls about leisurely and eats whatever comes its way. This way giants don't grow forever, even though they no longer die stupidly: the arena leader is about 2000 on easy, 3000 on medium and 4000–5000 on hard.
- **Big snakes don't hunt small ones** (`PREY_GAP`): a target at least 30% shorter than the bot is neither cut off nor ambushed, and if the target slims down during the chase, the bot drops it. Equals and smaller snakes do the hunting.
- **Bot anger depends on the level and the player's place** (`heatOf`, the level's `humanAggr` and `maxHunters`): weakest below 20th place, growing from 20th to first. In simulation (an autopilot player, hunts per minute at 20th → 1st place): easy 2 → 5, medium 5 → 9, hard 8 → 16; below the top 20 — 0 / 2 / 4.
- **Molts: length comes from mass, thickness from the stage** (`STAGES`, `STAGE_R`, `growStage`): at length thresholds of 100, 350, 900, 2200 and 5000 the snake molts and gets thicker (radius 13 → 20 → 28 → 38 → 47 → 56) — over 0.8 s, not in a jump. Between thresholds the thickness doesn't change, only the length grows. A stage is never lost during a life: slimmed down by boosting — shorter, but not thinner; after "Continue" the stage starts again by length. The step between beads still comes from mass (`spacingOf`), so the body length is the same as before.
- **Speed grows with the stage** (`speedOf`, by current length): a little one crawls at 210 px/s, +6% per stage, +30% from 5000. Boosting is 400 px/s, and a stage adds half as much to it, so a boosting little one still outruns a giant, but with a smaller margin.
- **A giant can be cut off, but it's harder**: it's faster, on medium and hard it predicts other heads further ahead (`foresee`), and a smaller share of it is left as remains (`remainsOf`): three quarters of the length for a little one, about 30% for a snake of 1000, about 18% for 4000, so you can't feast much off a dead giant.
- **Boosting is expensive and situational** (`boostCostOf`): every second burns 5% of the whole length, at least 10 — 50 per second for a snake of 1000, 200 for 4000. Only half of what's spent is left behind as food, so you can't boost non-stop: a snake of 1500 slims down to about 500 in half a minute of boosting even while collecting food along the way (without food — to 335). Below 20 boosting isn't available. Bots boost rarely — at the decisive moment of a hunt and toward rich food, if a rival is near and the food pays for the boost.
- **Sharper turning** (`turnOf`): a little one turns around in under half a second, a giant turns almost twice as fast as it used to. The joystick is shorter (44 px) — the finger travels less for a U-turn.

The balance is checked by simulation: an autopilot player and a count of what gets on screen (scripts outside the repository).

## Bots

Bots don't dart from crumb to crumb; they behave like rivals with intentions (`think` in `public/core.js`). On every think, in order:

1. **Threat.** If another head is about to cross the bot's path, it notices in advance and moves away along that path or away from it instead of crashing. Not every bot notices: on easy roughly one bot in ten, on medium one in five, on hard almost all (`wary`), and every bot has its own caution too.
2. **Hunt.** Cuts off its target from the side with a lead; boosts only once it's level, not during the whole chase. A second hunter comes in from the other side — a pincer; near the arena edge the bot approaches from the center side and pins the target against the wall. No catching up within 3 seconds — it gives up; it dodges other threats without dropping its target.
3. **Ambush.** If a player crawls across the bot's body by themselves, the bot doesn't turn and stretches its body out, boosting if needed. An ambush counts as a hunt: together with the hunters, no more than `maxHunters` bots go after a player at once (by the player's place); nobody ambushes a much shorter snake.
4. **Food.** Picks a rich spot (a grid cell with food), not a single crumb, and sticks to that goal until a clearly better one shows up; doesn't graze near a big snake's head. Bots gather to fresh remains from afar — after a giant falls, the area gets crowded.

A bot makes a decision about an encounter (noticed a threat, sets an ambush, goes for the remains) once and keeps it for 4 seconds — no jerking back and forth. Then it steers around bodies, the edge and other heads with rays (`rayDanger`):

- **the path to each heading is an arc**, with the bot's own turn rate, not a straight ray: the bot doesn't pick a sharp turn that won't get it away from a body in front of its nose in time;
- **bodies are seen whole**, even thick ones lying with their middle in a neighboring grid cell — so a bot doesn't crash into a lying body out of nowhere;
- **a bot notices a cut-off with a delay** (the level's `fresh`: 0.8 s on easy, 0.5 on medium, 0.18 on hard). It always sees the player's body whole. But if the player's tail has just been laid across its path right under its nose (`CUT_NEW`; closer than the level's `cutNear` edge to edge: 200 px on easy and medium, 110 on hard), it doesn't notice that piece of tail for that long. It sees the player's head, the rest of the body and other snakes as usual. Decided at first sight: a tail noticed from afar is avoided and no longer "missed". Nor is it missed by a bot that is hunting this player itself or that the player is coming at head-on; bots' tails have no delay either. Bots hunt each other by level (`feud`: 50% of hunting attempts on easy, 30% on medium, 6% on hard);
- **reaction by level** (`react` — the pause between thinks, `turn` — turn rate): 0.7 s on easy, 0.5 on medium, 0.08 on hard. A classic cut-off (overtake a bot 80–160 px to its side and turn across) succeeds in simulation 55–80% of the time on easy, about half the time on medium and almost never on hard;
- **lapses** (`slip` — times per minute: 6 on medium and 0.5 on hard, only when other heads are near or a chase is on; 12 on easy and anywhere — `slipAny`): the bot sees only right under its nose (`SLIP_LOOK`), misses threats and other heads, and thinks three times less often (`SLIP_REACT`) — it comes to only at its next think, so a lapse lasts about 1–2 s on medium and up to 2.7 s on easy. This is how collisions happen in the crush around food and in chases, and on easy — "silly" crashes into lying bodies too. Nobody lapses at the arena edge.
- **the crowned bot is careful** (`carefulOf`, `CAREFUL` in `core.js`): the longest bot thinks with a brain halfway from its level toward the hard level — quicker reactions, farther eyes, notices cut-offs sooner, lapses half as often (and only in crowds) and seldom hunts other bots; speed, turning and anger at players stay the level's. Over 6 worlds × 5 min the crown holds about a minute on easy and medium instead of ~15 s (half the changes used to be the leader getting eaten), so you have time to crawl up to it; the leader after 8 minutes is about as long on medium and ~25% longer on easy.
- **no newcomer takes a player's crown** (`spawnCap`, `SPAWN_BELOW` in `core.js`): while a player is in the top three, a bot appearing mid-game is at most 90% of the shortest crowned player's length. A crown can still be lost to a snake that grew or hunted in the game. Kept in third place for 3 minutes, a player used to see about 11 newcomers longer than them on easy and medium (up to twice as long); now none.

## Butterflies

40 butterflies fly around the arena — two brackets with what the butterfly has collected visible between them (`FLY_*` and `stepFlies` in `public/core.js`).

- **They collect food themselves:** they fly to rich food nearby and suck it in. Inside the brackets are crumbs, from one to five: their colors are what the butterfly ate most of, and the crumbs grow as it fills up. The brackets shimmer in the crumbs' colors. The butterfly itself grows; a full one (100) stops eating.
- **Nimble:** if a snake's head is close and facing the butterfly, it darts sideways — across the snake's course, toward the side with more room; after a dart it needs less than half a second of rest. A fat one flies and dodges slower. A little player catches more easily (`flyEase`, up to length 900): the butterfly notices the head later, dodges slower and less often, and the edge of the head is enough; bots get no such break.
- **They can't fly through snakes:** they bounce off bodies, so it pays to pin a butterfly with your body or against the edge. Only the head catches it.
- **A caught one spills everything it collected as food** in the crumbs' colors (plus 3 on top); the catcher gets a sound and stars. Big snakes get more from a butterfly (`flyGain`): 1.5× from length 700, 2× from 1300. A new, empty one arrives a few seconds later. Bots chase a butterfly only if it's near and already well fed.

At the start of a game the butterflies have already eaten — some more, some less. Checked by simulation: an autopilot player with human reaction time spends 10 to 30 seconds chasing one butterfly (a wide spread; a little one of length 15 couldn't catch any without the break, with it — about 8 per minute, the same as a snake of 150), and bots across the arena catch about 6–8 butterflies per minute.

## Interface

Minimalist: the home screen is a single page without scrolling: "?" ("How to play"), stars, a big snake (crawling head first) with arrows on the sides, below it the type, the skin and colored swatches of the skins, difficulty as tabs, a big yellow "Play" and "Play together" below it. The colors are dark blue (Material 3 from blue); the main button of the screen and of every dialog is yellow (`--cta`) so a child sees right away what to press; a name is asked only in "Play together" — alone your snake is always labeled "You". In the game: pause, length with place, and the top 3 with your own row. Sound is in the pause menu; controls, offline status and the version are in "How to play" (the "?" icon on the home screen); the controls hint is shown once, in the first game.

**Updating by button.** A new version downloads in the background and waits; the home screen shows a "A game update is ready" banner with an "Update" button — an update never cuts a match short. An open game asks the site for a new version when you return to the tab and once an hour. Without the button the new version takes over by itself when the game is closed and reopened.

**Spawning.** The player (at the start and after a crash) and bots arriving mid-game crawl out of a point instead of appearing whole: a long body never lands across someone's path.

**Feel: sound, picture, vibration.** All without text over the game.
- Volume: a slider in the pause menu and the room menu (`save.vol`; gain is the slider squared, before a compressor). On first launch it is at 50% (`VOL_DEF`, about −12 dB from full): full volume is uncomfortably loud. Saves from 0.23 and earlier with an untouched 100% are lowered to 50% once (`save.volV`).
- The arena backdrop (`public/backdrop.js`) is picked in the pause menu, "Backdrop": space (the default), grid, honeycomb, sea floor, sand, dots with colored patches, or a random one every game (never the same twice in a row). Its hue is a slider (-180…180°, all colors of the backdrop turn together; near-grey ones get a little color) or random every game (±40°, never close to last time's) — the default is space with a random hue. A strip on top of the dialog shows the choice and the arena behind changes at once; both are kept in the save (`bg`, `hue`). Every backdrop is three layers at different depths that move against each other as the camera travels (parallax): a layer's `k` is the share of the camera's movement it follows — 0 lies on the arena, above zero is below it and lags behind, below zero is above it and runs ahead. Space: nebulae and stars on the arena, distant stars at half speed, twinkling. Grid and honeycomb: glowing patches deep below, a wide lattice with a few lit cells, the arena's own lattice. Sea floor: the deep floor, a shelf of dark stones and seaweed, glints drifting over the arena. Sand: dunes and ripples on the arena, wind shadows and dust drifting above it. Dots: colored patches, out-of-focus lights, the arena's own marks. Nothing in a backdrop may pass for food, which is a small round glowing dot: stones are darker than the ground, plants are strokes, the out-of-focus lights are several times larger than the largest crumb, and the "dots" are small plus marks. Space is the exception: its stars are round dots, kept as they were — they are too small to be mixed up with food. To avoid visible "squares" of a repeating tile, the large patches (nebulae, shallows, dunes) are a single 768×768 canvas stretched over the whole arena (the blur makes the patches soft), laid out anew every game; the small stuff is a transparent tile on top, and a tile with noticeable things in it is laid turned by an angle so its copies don't line up with the screen. No more than three passes over the screen per frame. Clipping to the arena circle happens only when the edge is in view. `?bg=<kind>` and `?hue=<degrees>` in the URL pin them (for testing).
- A crumb's size follows its value (`foodLookOf`: area proportional to value, crumbs worth 1, 2 and 4 are distinguishable, boost drops are smaller); it can be eaten from the same distance as before (`foodRadiusOf` — balance is separate from the picture).
- Eating: a crumb near a head is pulled in no slower than `PULL_SPEED` — faster than any snake crawls or boosts, otherwise a crumb beside the head hung behind it on the neck and glowed from under the body; the crumb is sucked into the mouth, the head swells for a moment, a lump rolls down the body to the tail; food sounds in a row go slightly higher (crunch recordings — by 1.2% per crumb, the tonal "Yum" — along the pentatonic steps of `EAT_STEPS`), after a half-second pause they start from the bottom again.
- **Selectable sounds** (`public/sounds.js`): in the pause menu and the room menu there's "Sounds" with tabs "Food", "Boost", "Crash", "Molt"; a tap picks an option and plays it right away (food — three crumbs in a row, boost — a click and a second and a half of sound), the choice is `save.snd`. While the dialog is open in a room, the autopilot steers, as in the room menu.
  - Food (10) — real recorded crunches (synthesized ones sounded "pixelated"): cucumber, toast, apple, crisps, chomp, bunny, crouton and carrot (the same recordings, lower), munch-munch (two bites), and one tonal "Yum". The bites are cut into `public/sfx/eat.wav` (22050 Hz, mono, ~250 KB; the slice table is `EAT_SLICES`); a crumb is a random bite from the set with some pitch spread, slightly higher in a row, the previous bite fades out fast (`playBite`). The file loads on the first gesture; until it has loaded, "Yum" plays. The slicing was done with scripts outside the repository. Sources, all CC0 (OpenGameArt): "7 Eating Crunches" (starninjas), "Apple Bite" (antumdeluge), "Crunchy bite" (fvcalderan), "Crunch sounds (cucumber)" and "(burnt toast)" (cogitollc), "Rabbit Eating" (voltiment555).
  - Boost (9): a click when pressed and a sound while the bolt is held (`BOOST[].start`/`loop`, `startLoop`): wind, rocket, turbine, motor, bubbles, shhh, electro, space, click only.
  - Crash (7) and molt (7, each next stage a tone higher) — synthesized, fundamentals at 150–800 Hz, sawtooth and square through a filter.
- **Background music** (`MUSIC` in `public/sounds.js`, playback in `client.js`, "background music"): 22 tracks in `public/music/<id>.mp3`, picked in the pause menu and the room menu, "Music": its own volume (`save.musicVol`, 40% by default, 0 — off; it's also silent when the sound is off), "Random" (default; a new track after each one ends, never the same twice in a row; tapping it again skips to the next) or one track, which then repeats (`save.music`); the choice plays at once, in random mode a note marks the track playing. Gain is volume² × music volume² × 3: at the defaults music is about 10 dB under the effect peaks, at full music volume about level with them (measured on the output in Chromium). A track is fetched whole, decoded into an AudioBuffer at 32 kHz (as in the file — two thirds of the memory at 48 kHz; only the current track is kept, the old one is dropped before the next is decoded) and played by an AudioBufferSourceNode — pure Web Audio like the effects, with no `<audio>` element, so neither iPhone nor Android shows a "now playing" player on the lock screen (an `<audio>` did, in 0.28.0); the volume works on iPhone and the silent switch is obeyed. Minimizing stops it and remembers the second; a new audio context gets a new source on the same buffer from that second. The service worker keeps music in its own cache `snake-music-1` across versions; 20 s after start the remaining tracks are fetched one by one in the background, so all music is there offline; a track that fails to load is skipped for the session. Files: 32 kHz stereo 64 kbps, silence trimmed, loudness evened out (~−18 dB RMS, peaks ≤ −0.3 dB), tracks over 2.5 min cut with a fade; 14 MB in total. Prepared with scripts outside the repository (decoding in Chromium, encoding with LAME). Sources, all CC0 (OpenGameArt): "Happy Adventure (Loop)" (TinyWorlds), "Summer Park - 8bit tune (loop)" (Scribe), "Chill lofi inspired" (omfgdude), "Magic Space" (CodeManu), "Bossa Nova" (Joth), "Gone Fishin'" and "Beary Fishy" (memoraphile / You're Perfect Studio), "Carnival Rides" and "Meadow Thoughts" (Écrivain), "Underwater Theme" (CleytonKauffman), "Free Run [8 bit(ish)]" (TAD), "Doodle menu like song" (StumpyStrust), "Happy Lullaby", "Crystal Cave" and "Enchanted Tiki 86" (cynicmusic), "Talking Cute (Chiptune)" (Pro Sensory), "Chill Main Menu music" (HaelDB), and from "12 Music Loops" (SubspaceAudio): space upbeat, relaxed menu title, retro sports stage 3, storytime, super action stage 6.
- Boost: a click when the bolt is pressed and a sound while it's held (`setWhoosh`; the player picks the set, wind by default — a rustle that gets higher and brighter over a second and a half).
- A snake dying on screen: its beads scatter as sparks of its colors (`burst`); a caught butterfly — as pollen. Your own win — a short camera kick, in the solo game also a moment of slow motion (70 ms); your own death — a shake and the crash sound (picked by the player, see "Selectable sounds"). With the system "Reduce motion" setting there are no kicks or shakes.
- Vibration (`haptic`, a switch in the pause menu and the room menu, `save.haptic`) — Android only: `navigator.vibrate` with per-event patterns (`HAPTIC`), food and near misses no more than once every 110 ms. On iPhone the web can't vibrate (there's no `vibrate`, and Apple closed the `<input switch>` toggle loophole in iOS 26.5), so there's no switch there.

## What keeps it interesting

- **Molts** (instead of titles): the longer the snake, the thicker and fancier it is — growth shows on the snake itself, without text. Scales by stage (`plainBead`, `BEADS`, `dressBead` in `client.js`): 1 — simple flat beads (the head already has its type's look), 2 — volume and a glint, 3 — the type's pattern (spots, stripes, crest, lights), 4 — a light trim and a droplet glint, 5 — spikes along the spine and shimmer running along the body, 6 — golden trim and spikes, a soft golden glow and sparkles from the tail. The molt itself (`moltCheck`, about 1.3 s): a white glow runs from tail to head, then a wave rolls from head to tail — behind it the beads already have the new thickness and outfit, on the crest they swell and glow in the beads' colors; the old skin stays behind as a pale shadow and crumbles into sparks; the camera keeps the old zoom for a while, then smoothly pulls back; the molt sound (picked by the player, see "Selectable sounds": a rise while the glow runs and a chord when the snake swells; each next stage a tone higher); on Android — vibration; stars for the new stage. Other snakes on screen molt the same way, with a quieter sound from their side. In a room the stage travels in the snapshot (bits 2–4 of the snake flags) and from guest to host.
- **A place on the leaderboard** of 60 snakes: entering the top 10, the top 3 and first place gives the same golden wave; the top three wear gold, silver and bronze crowns. Places don't flicker between snakes of almost the same length: a snake takes the place above only once it's longer than that one by 3% plus 10 more (`stickyRank`, `RANK_HOLD` in `core.js`; in a room the host keeps the order, so everyone sees the same crowns). A crown doesn't jump back and forth while you boost or eat next to a rival, and the stars for a place come together with its crown; within that gap a row may show a slightly bigger number than the row above.
- **Stars** accumulate between games: for food, wins, caught butterflies, molts and places. Stars unlock snakes (in the game — a double golden wave; which one unlocked is shown in the summary). There are 8 snake types (`TYPES` in `core.js`): beads, fish, caterpillar, spotty, dragon, stripy, robot, neon; each type has its own bead (a droplet scale, legs, spots, a crest, a stripe, a cube with a light, a glowing ring) and its own head (fins, feelers, horns, ears, a screen with an antenna). Within a type there are skins: beads have 12 palettes, the others 4 each, 40 in total (`SKINS`; new ones go only at the end of the list: the skin index is stored in saves and sent over the network). The first skin of a type unlocks early (fish — at 100 stars), the rest are mixed in, the last one at 34,000. On the home screen the arrows and swiping the snake change the type (all 8 in a loop; a locked one shows "N ★ to go"), the colored swatches under the name are that type's skins (locked ones are dim with a lock); a selected locked skin shows its price and can't be played. While boosting, a wave of glow in the beads' colors runs from head to tail. Bots crawl around in locked skins too — so you see what to aim for. So you don't mistake your own snake for someone else's, bots don't take the types of the humans in the arena (if there are more humans than types — at least not similar skins, `SKIN_NEAR` — closeness of palettes of the same type in OKLab); when a new player joins a room, bots of their type get recolored, except those someone already sees.
- **Wins** without text: a flash where the snake was and a sound; a streak and a win over a giant sound brighter.
- **A golden feast** appears somewhere in the arena once a minute, and bots gather there; there's no arrow to it.
- **At the screen edge** — only the golden arrow with a crown pointing to the leaderboard leader (if the leader isn't you and isn't on screen).
- **Revenge**: after a crash the offender is circled in red (no arrow to it), and beating it gives more stars.

Progress is stored in the device's `localStorage` (key `zmeigrad` — the game's original name; kept so existing progress isn't lost).

A solo game survives the app being unloaded from memory: the whole arena (snakes with their bodies and characters, food, butterflies, the feast, timers — `World.toSave` / `World.fromSave` in `core.js`) plus the game's stars and places is written to `localStorage` (`zmeigrad-run:<game id>`, list in `zmeigrad-runs`) when the game goes to the background, on pause and every 30 s of play (about 250 KB, a few ms, only if the world moved since the last save). The next launch opens the game paused ("Continue" / "Back to menu"), or "Oops!" again if it was unloaded there; after a restore your snake has 1.5 s of invulnerability to look around. Every game has its own save, so parallel tabs don't overwrite each other (up to 3, the oldest go): a tab remembers its game in `sessionStorage` and gets it back when reloaded, a new launch takes the newest game not open in another tab (asked over a `BroadcastChannel`, 250 ms; a frozen tab doesn't answer, so its game may open twice), and "Back to menu", a new game or a room drop only the tab's own save. A save of another format (`SAVE_V`) or a broken one is dropped and the launch shows the menu; an invitation link (`?room=`) opens the menu and leaves saves for later.

## Architecture

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `public/core.js` | shared world logic: snakes, food, butterflies, bots, feast, collisions, skins and molts. No DOM and no network |
| `public/client.js` | rendering, controls, menus, stars; the solo game and the network client |
| `public/i18n.js` | language detection and `t()`; translation of the static page |
| `public/lang/*.js` | dictionaries for 13 languages; English is the reference and the fallback |
| `public/sounds.js` | selectable sound sets: food (crunch recordings), boost, crash, molt |
| `public/sfx/eat.wav` | crunch bites (CC0) for the food sounds |
| `public/backdrop.js` | arena backdrops and their hue turn: non-repeating patches and detail tiles |
| `public/sw.js` | offline cache; a new version installs in the background and takes over via the "Update" button |
| `public/room.js` | a shared-game room: it runs in the host's browser (40 world steps and 40 snapshots per second, each player's area of view) |
| `public/rtc.js` | direct connection between players (WebRTC): introduction via `/ws`, a reliable channel and one without retransmits, a loopback for the host |
| `server/server.js` | static files + introduction over WebSocket `/ws`: room codes and relaying a few messages on join. The server doesn't run the game |
| `server/ws.js` | minimal WebSocket (RFC 6455), no dependencies |

**Multiplayer without a game server.** Whoever creates a room is its host: their browser computes the world — bots, food, butterflies, the feast — with the same `core.js` as the solo game. Each player (the host included) computes their own snake locally (`selfStep` in `client.js`): a turn shows instantly, and players decide about their own crash — based on what they see on screen. A player sends the host where their snake is 40 times a second, and, reliably, what they ate, what they dropped while boosting, what they caught and what they crashed into (cheaters aren't a concern: the room trusts its players). In the host's world human snakes are "remote" (`remote`): the world doesn't move, feed or kill them, but bots see their bodies and crash into them — the win then goes to the player. The host plays the same way as guests — through a loopback inside the page.

The connection is direct WebRTC. The site is only needed for the introduction: a guest with the room code exchanges an offer, an answer and addresses with the host and disconnects from the site; the host keeps `/ws` open so new players can join (after a dropout it gets the same code back by token). The browser learns its address behind the router from a public STUN server (Cloudflare, Google); on the same home Wi-Fi the connection works without one. There's no relaying TURN server: on some mobile networks a direct connection can't be established — the player is then advised to use the same Wi-Fi. World snapshots go over the channel without retransmits (a lost snapshot doesn't hold up the next ones); food, events and the leaderboard go over the reliable, ordered one. The client draws other snakes 70 ms behind. Measured on one machine: turning your own snake takes 20–35 ms (previously 135–170 ms plus the trip to the server), a guest lags 10–30 ms behind in the host's world and ~100 ms on the host's screen. While the host has the game minimized, the room is frozen for everyone; when the host leaves, the room closes. The room menu doesn't pause the game: while it's open (and the boost button setup opened from it), a careful autopilot steers your snake (`safeCourse` in `core.js`): it tries 24 headings, plays out an arc path 1.6 s ahead and picks the one farthest from bodies, from heads projected forward and from the arena edge; no boosting, food only when there's plenty of room. Close the menu — the controls are yours right away (the joystick and mouse course starts from the current one). In a browser measurement over 3 minutes with the menu open: 0 crashes with the autopilot on easy and hard, 11–12 without it (on the last heading).

The host sends each player only the snakes with at least part of the body within 2300 of their head, butterflies within 2600, and food from 1000×1000 cells within a radius of 2600; once a second — the leaderboard for the whole arena. The client rebuilds bodies itself from the movement of the heads. Up to 8 players per room.

## Development

```bash
node server/server.js        # http://localhost:8080
node --test test/*.test.js   # tests for the world, rooms, the introduction and localization
```

Node 22+, no dependencies.

## Release

1. After changing files in `public/`, bump `VERSION` in `public/core.js` and `public/sw.js` (a test checks they match) — otherwise devices stay on the old cached version, and a room host won't let in a client of a different version.
2. Tag `vX.Y.Z` → GitHub Actions builds `ghcr.io/zxibizz/snake-game:X.Y.Z`, and Flux in the cluster picks up the new tag by itself.
